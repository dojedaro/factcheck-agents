# EU AI Act: Obligations on Providers of General-Purpose AI Models and When They Apply

> What obligations does the EU AI Act place on providers of general-purpose AI models, and when do they apply?

Under the EU AI Act, all providers of general-purpose AI (GPAI) models must keep technical documentation [F1], inform downstream providers [F2], maintain a copyright-compliance policy [F3], publish a summary of training content [F4], and, if established outside the EU, appoint an EU authorised representative [F5]. Providers of GPAI models with systemic risk face additional duties, reportedly including model evaluations, adversarial testing, systemic-risk assessment and mitigation, and cybersecurity protections [F8][F9]. These obligations entered into application on 2 August 2025 [F12], but the Commission will enforce them, including through fines, only from 2 August 2026 [F13]. Models already on the market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027 to comply [F14][F10].

## Baseline obligations for all GPAI providers

Every GPAI provider must draw up and keep up to date technical documentation of the model, including its training and testing process and evaluation results [F1]. One source adds that this must include at least the Annex XI information and be supplied to the AI Office and national authorities on request, but that detail is only partly supported by the evidence [F1]. Providers must also give information and documentation to downstream AI-system providers so those providers understand the model's capabilities and limitations and can meet their own obligations [F2]. They must implement a policy to comply with EU copyright and related rights, including identifying and respecting rights reservations with state-of-the-art technologies [F3]. They must publicly release a sufficiently detailed summary of training content using the AI Office template (Art. 53(1)(d)) [F4]. Providers established outside the EU must appoint an authorised representative in the Union before placing their model on the market [F5].

## Additional obligations for GPAI models with systemic risk

Providers of GPAI models with systemic risk must carry out standardised model evaluations and adversarial testing [F8]. They must also assess and mitigate systemic risk and put cybersecurity protections in place [F9]. Both points come from a secondary summary site rather than the legal text [F8][F9]. Article 53 includes an open-source exception, but it explicitly does not apply to GPAI models with systemic risk [F6].

## Demonstrating compliance

Providers may rely on codes of practice under Article 56 to demonstrate compliance [F7]. One source further states that this applies until a harmonised standard is published, and that providers using neither route must show other adequate means of compliance to the Commission, but the evidence only partly supports these details [F7]. A voluntary GPAI Code of Practice was reportedly published in July 2025 and signed by most major developers, though this claim is only partly verified [F15].

## Timeline of application and enforcement

The AI Act entered into force on 1 August 2024, with obligations phased in over time [F15]. The Commission states that GPAI provider obligations entered into application on 2 August 2025 [F12], and the GPAI chapter (Articles 51–56) is described as applying from that date [F10]. The Commission will enforce full compliance, including through fines, only from 2 August 2026 [F13]. Commentary written after that date reports that the AI Office can now fine GPAI providers up to 3% of global turnover; its framing that the obligations were binding but unenforceable from August 2025 is only partly supported [F16]. Models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027 to comply (Art. 111(3)) [F14][F10].

## Open questions

Commentators have asked who carries GPAI provider obligations when a downstream actor fine-tunes or modifies a model: the original provider, the modifier, or both [F17]. The evidence does not resolve this question [F17].

## Limitations

- The evidence does not say which obligations the open-source exception waives, only that it does not cover systemic-risk models.
- No evidence covers how a GPAI model is classified as having systemic risk, such as compute thresholds or Commission designation, or any notification duties.
- Systemic-risk obligations rest on a secondary summary site, not the legal text, and the evidence may not list them all (for example, incident reporting is not covered).
- Several claims are only partly verified: the Annex XI and on-request details of the documentation duty, the harmonised-standard and alternative-means details for codes of practice, the Code of Practice publication and signatories, and the post-2026 enforcement commentary.
- The 3% fine figure comes from a single secondary commentary source and is not confirmed by the Commission guidance in the evidence.
- The allocation of obligations after fine-tuning or modification remains unresolved in the evidence.
